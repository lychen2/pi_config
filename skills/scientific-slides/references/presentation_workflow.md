# Presentation Development Workflow

Apply only the stages needed for the requested deliverable. Writing notes for an
existing deck does not require new literature research, image generation, or rebuilding.

## 1. Establish context

Use available information to determine audience, talk type, duration, venue, format,
and key message. Ask only for missing decisions that block progress.

Read the supplied deck, figures, results, and references. Search for additional
literature when context or claim support is missing, without a fixed paper quota.
Use available literature tools; do not assume a particular skill name is installed.

Identify a small set of takeaways and allocate time to the material that supports them.
An outline might proceed from question to methods, results, interpretation, and
conclusion, but adapt the sequence to the audience and evidence.

## 2. Choose implementation

Follow the requested format and existing project conventions:

- PowerPoint for editable decks or institutional templates.
- Beamer for LaTeX-based content; see `beamer_guide.md`.
- Optional image generation for suitable visual material; see `prompt_writing.md`
  and `script_reference.md`. Obtain authorization before external transfer of
  confidential inputs. Render quantitative data faithfully, not by generative redraw.

Use actual authorship and affiliation details. Templates and example prompts do not
supply facts about the user's work.

## 3. Develop slides and notes

Give each slide a clear message and the evidence needed to understand it. Prefer
legible research figures, concise labels, and layouts that fit the material. Do not
force a word count, bullet count, visual quota, or decorative graphic onto every slide.

Write speaker notes as natural spoken explanation, not bullet text or an audit log:

- Explain the result, comparison, or method that matters on this slide.
- Define shared metrics, evaluation conditions, and figure conventions once; later
  slides should focus on what differs.
- Preserve uncertainty and conditions that change interpretation, including simulation
  status, optimization budget differences, and metric denominators.
- State corrected facts directly. Omit earlier mistakes, instructions followed, export
  repairs, and repeated denials of claims not made by the talk.
- Keep normal citations. Detailed local paths and provenance records belong in separate
  reference material when needed, not as a repeated source footer in spoken notes.

## 4. Validate the deliverable

Check agreement between sources, figures, slides, and notes. Check values, units,
labels, citations, terminology, and conclusion strength. Do not invent missing data.

For rendered slides, follow `visual_review_workflow.md`: inspect images for clipping,
overlap, readability, and contrast; fix observed issues and inspect affected slides
again. For a notes-only revision, check text and its agreement with the deck instead.

Keep validation records separate from audience content. Report a material unresolved
issue honestly, without adding a compliance checklist to the talk.

## 5. Refine timing and delivery

Estimate timing from the script and planned pauses, or use actual rehearsal results
when available. Do not claim a practice run, projector test, or audience test that was
not performed. Cut lower-priority material before rushing the conclusion.

Read the notes from the audience's perspective. Remove production commentary and
repetitive caveats, retain meaningful limitations, and smooth transitions.

## 6. Finalize

Deliver the requested files or text in the agreed format. Include backup formats or
appendices when needed, not by default. Briefly report actual checks and unresolved
issues outside the talk. Physical venue checks remain presenter actions unless the
agent has evidence they were completed.
